## Threat actor types (Source: Pt2 p.29)

- **Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)** — state sponsored actors or nation-state actors.
  - **Advanced** — targeted, coordinated, purposeful.
  - **Persistent** — month after month, year after year.
  - **Threat** — person(s) with intent, opportunity, and capability.
- **Hacktivist** — might attempt to obtain and release confidential information to the public domain, perform denial of service (DoS) attacks, or deface websites; often influence social change, promote a political agenda, even cyberterrorism (hacking for a cause).
- **Criminal syndicates** — (organized criminals and/or terrorist groups) who use hacking tools, such as ransomware, for financial gain and can operate across the Internet from different jurisdictions than its victim, increasing the complexity of prosecution.
- **Competitors** — may use hacking tools and techniques for corporate espionage purposes.
- **Insider threats / malicious insider** — a current or former employee, contractor or business partner who has or had authorized access to an organization's network, system or data and intentionally exceeded or misused that access in a manner that negatively affected the CIA of the organization's information systems.
- **Shadow IT** — users purchase or introduce computer hardware or software to the workplace without the sanction of the IT department and without going through a procurement and security analysis process.
- **Unskilled hackers (script kiddies)** — unsophisticated individual with little or no skills when it comes to technology and uses code written by others to leverage their attacks.

(Source: Pt2 p.29)

## Typical hackers (hat colors) (Source: Pt2 p.30)

| Type | Slide definition |
|---|---|
| White hats (authorized) | Non-malicious security professionals who attempt to "hack" into a computer system to test the security controls; have permission |
| — Ethical hacker | A white hat that has been given permission from the system owner to conduct system attacks, penetration testing, and intrusion testing for the purpose of testing the system, security controls, and security processes |
| Gray hats (semi-authorized) | Hackers that may risk breaking the law by hacking systems, however, inform the administrator of the weaknesses they discovered; may also be reformed black hats that now use their skills to help protect systems |
| Black hats (unauthorized) | Malicious hackers who attempt to break into computer systems/networks without authorization to damage, corrupt, or compromise the system; attempt identity theft, piracy, credit card fraud, etc. |

(Source: Pt2 p.30. Three hat pictures, decorative.)

## Attributes of actors (Source: Pt2 p.31)

- **Internal/External** — attacks might come from an internal or external threat source. External attacks have always been devastating to organizations. Internal threats are even more dangerous given the level of legitimate access to system and resources.
- **Resources/funding** — no money; extensive funding.
- **Level of sophistication and capability** — attackers also differ in their level of sophisticated techniques, tactics, and procedures (TTPs) and capability of their tools they possess. Ability to craft novel exploit techniques and tools; ability to write their own code.
  - **Techniques, Tactics, and Procedures (TTPs)** — a generalized statement of adversary behaviors in terms of campaign strategy and approach (tactics), generalized attack vectors (techniques), and specific intrusion tools and methods (procedures).

(Source: Pt2 p.31)

## Intent and motivation (Source: Pt2 p.32)

- **Intent** — describes what an attacker hopes to achieve from the attack: vandalizing or disrupting a system, or stealing.
- **Motivation** — the attacker's reason for perpetrating the attack; driven by greed, curiosity, or some sort of grievance. The slide lists: data exfiltration; espionage; service disruption; blackmail; financial gain; philosophical/political beliefs; ethical; revenge; disruption/chaos; war.
- The slide lists the ten motivation terms without defining each one individually.

(Source: Pt2 p.32)
