## Vendor Assessment (Source: Pt7 p.8)

- **Vendor Assessment** — evaluating third-party vendor capabilities, practices, and security measures *before* engaging in business partnerships or entrusting them with sensitive data and critical services. (Source: Pt7 p.8)
  - A critical component of Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) frameworks. (Source: Pt7 p.8)
  - Plays a pivotal role in maintaining the security of IT and business operations. (Source: Pt7 p.8)
- **Vendor assessment methods** (Source: Pt7 p.8):
  - **Penetration Testing** — evaluates the vendor's security posture and identifies potential vulnerabilities in their systems, networks, and applications.
  - **Right-to-Audit Clause** — a contractual provision that grants the organization authority to conduct audits or assessments of the vendor's operational practices, information systems, and security controls.
  - **Evidence of Internal Audits** — looking for evidence that the vendor has internal audit practices.
  - **Independent Assessments** — engaging independent experts to evaluate and verify vendor capabilities, security, and compliance practices.
  - **Supply Chain Analysis** — the interconnected network of entities involved in producing, distributing, and delivering goods or services, from raw material suppliers to manufacturers, distributors, retailers, and ultimately the end customer.

## Vendor Selection (Source: Pt7 p.9)

- **Vendor Selection** — systematically evaluating and assessing potential vendors to minimize risks associated with outsourcing or procurement. (Source: Pt7 p.9)
  - Identify risk criteria → conduct due diligence → select vendors based on their risk profile. (Source: Pt7 p.9)
- **Due Diligence** — the comprehensive and systematic process of gathering and analyzing information about potential vendors to assess their suitability, reliability, and integrity. (Source: Pt7 p.9)
- **Conflict of Interest** — when an individual or organization has investments or obligations that could compromise their ability to act objectively, impartially, or in the best interest of another party. (Source: Pt7 p.9)
  - Types listed: financial interests; personal relationships; competitive relationships; insider information. (Source: Pt7 p.9)

## Agreement Types (Source: Pt7 p.10-11)

| Agreement | Slide definition | Key distinguisher | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SLA** — Service Level Agreement | Contractual agreement setting out the detailed terms under which a service is provided; spells out expectations | Between a **client and service provider** | Pt7 p.10 |
| **ISA** — Interconnection Security Agreement | A security risk awareness process; commits the agency and supplier to implementing security controls | Any **federal agency** interconnecting its IT system to a third party must create an ISA to govern the relationship | Pt7 p.10 |
| **NDA** — Non-Disclosure Agreement | Entities will not share confidential information, knowledge, or materials with unauthorized third parties | Violations may face **legal consequences** | Pt7 p.10 |
| **SOW / WO** — Statement of Work / Work Order | Details a vendor project or engagement's scope, deliverables, timelines, and responsibilities | Project-specific (see below) | Pt7 p.10 |
| **MOU / MOA** — Memorandum of Understanding / Agreement | A preliminary or exploratory agreement to express an intent to work together; the intended actions and expectations towards a common goal | Between **business parties** | Pt7 p.11 |
| **BPA** — Business Partnership Agreement | Agreement by two companies to work together closely (e.g., partner agreements large IT companies set up with resellers and solution providers); establishes terms, conditions, and expectations | Between **business partners** | Pt7 p.11 |
| **MSA** — Master Service Agreement | Contract establishing the fundamental agreements between two parties | Between **two parties**; also known as a **framework agreement** | Pt7 p.11 |

- SOW/WO details (Source: Pt7 p.10):
  - Clarifies the vendor's tasks, the organization's expectations, the agreed-upon deliverables, and the acceptance criteria.
  - The narrative description of a project's work requirement.
  - Defines project-specific activities, deliverables, and timelines for a vendor providing services to the client.
- MSA details (Source: Pt7 p.11):
  - Lets vendors and clients agree on basic terms at the outset, which can drastically speed up negotiation for **future projects and contracts**.
  - Parties agree to most of the terms that will govern **future transactions or future agreements**.
- The slides treat MOU/MOA as one combined entry; they are not differentiated. (Source: Pt7 p.11)

## Vendor Monitoring (Source: Pt7 p.12)

- **Vendor Monitoring** — continuously overseeing and evaluating vendors to ensure ongoing adherence to: security standards, compliance requirements, contractual obligations. (Source: Pt7 p.12)
- Allows organizations to promptly identify and address potential risks or issues via: regular performance reviews; periodic assessments; real-time monitoring of vendor activities. (Source: Pt7 p.12)

## Questionnaires (Source: Pt7 p.13)

- **Questionnaires** — a structured means of obtaining consistent information, enabling more effective risk analysis and comparison. (Source: Pt7 p.13)
- Gather vendor information on: security policies, procedures, and practices; security controls; data protection; access management; incident response; disaster recovery; risk management strategies. (Source: Pt7 p.13)
- Help organizations: assess a vendor's security posture; identify a vendor's vulnerabilities; evaluate a vendor's capabilities. (Source: Pt7 p.13)

## Rules of Engagement (RoE) (Source: Pt7 p.14)

- **Rules of Engagement (RoE)** — documentation that defines the purpose (objective) of a security test, test boundaries (scope), assumptions and limitations, risks, personnel involved, reporting, test schedule, test processes, and test site and equipment. (Source: Pt7 p.14)
  - A definition of how a pen test will be executed and what constraints will be in place.
  - Outlines the responsibilities, communication methods, reporting mechanisms, security requirements, and compliance obligations that vendors must adhere to.
  - Provides the pen tester with guidelines to consult while conducting tests.
  - Scope or limits of the tests: dates and times testing will be performed; what IP addresses the tester will use; what devices or web applications are in scope (IPs and URLs).
- Callout bar at the bottom of the slide: **"What activity is permitted or not permitted"**. (Source: Pt7 p.14)
