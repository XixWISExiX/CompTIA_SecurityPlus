## Phishing Campaigns (Source: Pt7 p.49)

- **Phishing Campaigns** — internal training exercise that sends simulated phishing messages to users; users who respond to the messages can be targeted for follow-up training. (Source: Pt7 p.49)
- Purposes listed (Source: Pt7 p.49):
  - Raises awareness and educates employees about the risks and consequences of falling victim to such attacks
  - Enhance threat awareness
  - Protect sensitive information
  - Mitigate social engineering risks
  - Promote incident response
  - Strengthen security practices
- Enables employees to respond appropriately to phishing attempts (Source: Pt7 p.49):
  - Reporting incidents to specific IT or security teams
  - Refraining from clicking suspicious links or opening attachments
  - Verifying requests sent via email
- Training employees to recognize and respond to phishing attempts strengthens an organization's cybersecurity defenses (Source: Pt7 p.49):
  - Cultivates a culture of security awareness
  - Empowers employees to protect sensitive information actively
  - Enhances the organization's resilience against evolving threats

## Anomalous Behavior Recognition (Source: Pt7 p.50)

- **Anomalous Behavior Recognition** — actions or patterns that deviate significantly from expectations. (Source: Pt7 p.50)
  - Systems that automatically detect users, hosts, and services that deviate from what is expected, or systems and training that encourage reporting of this by employees.
  - Examples: unusual network traffic, user account activity anomalies, insider threat actions, abnormal system events, and fraudulent transactions.

| Type | Slide definition | Examples on the slide |
|---|---|---|
| **Risky Behaviors** | Actions or practices that threaten data security, systems, or networks | Unsafe online activities: clicking suspicious links, visiting untrusted websites, downloading unauthorized software. Neglecting security measures: using weak passwords, sharing credentials, ignoring software updates |
| **Unexpected Behaviors** | Actions that deviate from established security protocols or violate security policies | Lack of awareness, carelessness, or failure to follow established procedures. Unauthorized access to sensitive information, bypassing security controls, or disregarding physical security measures |
| **Unintentional Behaviors** | Actions without malicious intent but that can still have detrimental consequences | Human error, lack of training, or lack of understanding of security best practices. Accidental data breaches, mishandling of confidential information, or falling victim to social engineering attacks |

(Source: Pt7 p.50)

## Development of Training (Source: Pt7 p.53)

- Content development focuses on creating engaging and informative training materials. (Source: Pt7 p.53)
  - Using clear language
  - Incorporating real-world examples to enhance relevance
  - Interactive elements: quizzes, case studies, or simulations
  - Encourage: active participation, critical thinking, and practical application of knowledge
  - Facilitating: dialogue, discussion, and question-and-answer sessions
  - Regular reviews and updates to training materials: ensure content remains relevant and aligned with evolving security threats

## Diversity of Training Techniques (Source: Pt7 p.54)

(This slide is not named in the Section Topics list; it sits between Development and Execution.)

- **Role-Based Training** — defining a unique approach to identifying and customizing training based on the specific roles and functions within the organization. (Source: Pt7 p.54)
- **Computer-Based Training (CBT)** — use of a personal or networked computer for delivery and access of training programs; CBT might use video game elements to improve engagement. (Source: Pt7 p.54)
  - **Simulations** — recreating system interfaces or using emulators so participants can practice configuration tasks
  - **Branching Scenarios** — participants choose between options to find the best choices to solve a cybersecurity incident or configuration problem
- **Capture the Flag (CTF)** — usually used in ethical hacker training programs and gamified competitions; participants complete a series of challenges within a virtualized computing environment to discover a flag or token. (Source: Pt7 p.54)
  - Red team vs Blue team exercise
- **Gamification** — a competitive challenge of CTF events that can also be used to boost security awareness for other roles. (Source: Pt7 p.54)
  - Taking game mechanics and applying them to business objectives
  - Receive points for reporting suspicious emails; helps with motivation factor
  - Competition, winning, or a sense of accomplishment; like receiving digital badges

## Execution of Training (Source: Pt7 p.55)

- Successfully executing security training means effectively providing education and instruction to employees and staff that enhance their knowledge, skills, and awareness of security practices. (Source: Pt7 p.55)
  - Addressing relevant security topics: data protection; incident response; phishing awareness; secure coding practices; physical security
  - Assess the effectiveness of security awareness practices: collecting feedback; conducting assessments; developing relevant measurements and metrics (help gauge the impact of the training and identify areas for improvement)

## Reporting and Monitoring (Source: Pt7 p.56)

- Initial and recurring effectiveness measurements are crucial to gauge the overall impact of security awareness training. (Source: Pt7 p.56)

| Measurement | Slide definition | Detail bullets |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial** | The immediate impact of security awareness training of participants | Measures knowledge gained, awareness raised, and behavioral changes observed immediately after completing a training program; provides insight into how participants absorbed the information and concepts; pre- and post-training assessments, quizzes, and surveys to gauge understanding before and after training |
| **Recurring** | The long-term impact and sustainability of security awareness training of participants | Measures continued behavioral changes and the level of security consciousness over an extended period; examines whether participants retained and applied knowledge and skills day-to-day; ensures the training has a lasting effect and leads to sustained improvements in security practices |

(Source: Pt7 p.56)
